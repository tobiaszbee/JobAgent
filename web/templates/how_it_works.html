From a PDF to a ranked shortlist

JobAgent isn't a search box — it's a small pipeline that reads your CV, learns what you're looking for, and reads hundreds of postings a day so you only have to look at the ones worth your time. Here's exactly what happens at each step.

01 · Getting started 02 · Searching, every day 03 · How a posting gets evaluated 04 · Getting smarter over time
01

Getting started

Two things happen once, before any searching begins: your CV becomes a profile, and you tell the agent what actually matters to you.

Your CV becomes a profile

You upload a PDF. AI reads it and pulls out the things that matter for matching: your tech stack, years of experience, seniority level, and a short summary — the same way a recruiter would skim it, just faster and every time consistently.

You answer a few questions

Your CV can't tell the agent your salary floor, whether you'll do hybrid, or which technologies you'd rather avoid — so a short questionnaire fills in the rest. Every answer is editable later, and nothing here is guessed.

03

How a posting gets evaluated

Every posting goes through the same pipeline, in order. The early steps are free and instant — they exist specifically so the expensive AI reading only happens on postings that have a real chance.

Fast, free filtering no AI cost

Before any AI reads a word, three quick checks run: is this posting written in a language you actually speak, does it mention anything on your "avoid" list or miss a must-have skill, and does it obviously break a hard rule — pay below your floor, or on-site when you said remote-only. Anything that fails is set aside immediately, for free.

AI reads and scores what's left

Everything that survives gets read in full by AI and scored 0–10 against your profile. You don't just get a number — you get a breakdown: what fits well, what's a concern, and separate mini-scores for stack, seniority, company type, and pay, so you can see exactly why a posting landed where it did.

The best matches get ranked against each other

Scoring each posting alone only gets you so far — "good" is relative. So the strongest candidates go through a second pass where AI compares them directly against one another and settles on a definitive best-to-worst order, the way you'd sort a shortlist by hand if you had time to read all of them side by side.

A second, independent AI double-checks it

A skill match can look great on paper and still hide a dealbreaker — the wrong seniority, the wrong company type — especially when the stack lines up almost perfectly. A separate review pass looks specifically for that pattern and can bump a posting down (or flag it as underrated) before you ever see the list.

Why two AI passes, not one: a single model can be consistently fooled by the same kind of mismatch. A second, independent read — checking the first one's work rather than starting over — catches what one pass alone tends to miss, without the cost of scoring everything twice from scratch.
Most postings never reach the expensive steps. The free filtering stage typically clears out well over half of what gets collected — so the AI reading time (and cost) goes toward postings that actually have a shot.
04

Getting smarter over time

You just apply, reject, or mark postings as reviewed — same as you'd do anywhere else. Two things happen quietly in the background because of it.

Learns from what you do

Not just what you said

Your stated preferences (the questionnaire) are one signal. What you actually apply to and reject is another — and sometimes they disagree. AI periodically studies your real decisions — company type, pay patterns, contract form, culture cues in the postings you liked vs. rejected — and builds a second, learned profile that feeds into every future score and ranking, alongside your stated answers.

Learns from its own mistakes

Calibration

When the ranking puts something near the top and you reject it — or ranks something low and you apply anyway — that's a real disagreement worth remembering. Those specific cases get fed back into future scoring as a direct "you got this wrong before, don't repeat it" note, so the same mistake doesn't just happen quietly forever.

That's the whole loop — upload once, answer a few questions, and it runs every day from there.
